    About the project

    A lander at the lunar south pole only gets power when the Sun rises above the surrounding peaks. Near the pole the Sun skims the horizon, so a ridge a few hundred metres high can hide it for days.

    We can't test the prediction on the Moon, so we test it on Earth: a camera device in the mountains records the real sunrise every morning, and its log is overlaid on the simulated sunrise to measure the error.
